About

I am an undergraduate at Yale University pursuing a B.S. in Computer Science and Mathematics. My research focuses on vision-language-action (VLA) models, which map visual observations and natural-language instructions to robot actions, and on vision-language models more broadly.

At the APOLLO Lab, I design controlled experiments that diagnose language-grounding failures in VLA policies, contributing to a co-authored paper on B-spline analysis. I also conduct research on vision-language models and generative video with Gan Chuang. Previously, I studied industrial robotics and automation with Mitsubishi Electric at MassRobotics (2022–2025), developed retrieval-augmented generation systems at Boston University’s Software & Application Innovation Lab, and completed the MITx MicroMasters program in Data, Economics, and Design of Policy.

Outside of research, I practice jiǎnzhǐ (Chinese paper cutting); my work has been exhibited since 2018 and received a Scholastic Art Award Gold Key. I sing tenor with Magevet at Yale and serve as Junior Treasurer of the Yale Political Union. My broader interests include philosophy, religion, literature, political thought, and Chinese opera.

Interactive Demonstrations

Two browser-based demonstrations drawn from my work in art and in research.

Drag to cut

Studio

Fold & cut

In traditional paper cutting, a single cut through folded paper is repeated around the sheet. This demonstration reproduces the process computationally: each stroke is mapped through the dihedral group Dn, comprising n rotations and n reflections.

Lab

B-spline playground

B-splines represent a smooth curve with a small set of control points, which makes them a compact representation of robot trajectories and the subject of my co-authored APOLLO Lab paper. Each control point influences only a local segment of the curve; select a point to display its basis function and region of support.
