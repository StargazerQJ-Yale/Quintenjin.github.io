Language, motion & negative space.

I’m a Computer Science and Mathematics student at Yale. My research is where language meets motion: vision-language-action models that turn an instruction into a robot’s movement, and vision-language models more broadly.

Right now I’m running controlled experiments to find where and why language grounding fails in robot policies. Before Yale, I spent four years studying industrial robotics with Mitsubishi Electric at MassRobotics, built retrieval-augmented systems at Boston University’s Software & Application Innovation Lab, and completed MITx’s MicroMasters in Data, Economics, and Design of Policy.

Away from the keyboard I make jiǎnzhǐ, Chinese paper cuts, and have exhibited since 2018. I sing tenor in Magevet, have hosted and performed at music festivals from Boston to Belgrade, and am always up for a conversation about philosophy, religion, literature, politics, or Chinese opera.

Workshop.

Two small things to play with, one from the studio and one from the lab. Both run entirely in your browser.

Drag to cut

Studio

Fold & cut

Many paper cuts start with a fold: one snip through the stack repeats all the way around. This studio does the same with a little group theory. Every stroke is copied through the dihedral group Dn: n rotations and n reflections.

Lab

B-spline playground

B-splines turn a handful of control points into a smooth curve. They are a compact way to describe a robot trajectory, and the subject of my co-authored APOLLO Lab paper. Each point pulls on only a local stretch of the curve: hover or drag one to see its basis function and the span it controls.

Drag points · double-click to add or remove · Tab to a point, then use the arrow keys
